## How We May Use and Disclose Your Health Information

The following describes how we may use and disclose your PHI. Not every use or disclosure is listed here, but all uses and disclosures fall into one of these categories.

### 1. Treatment
We may use your PHI to provide, coordinate, or manage your care and related services. For example, your therapist may share information with a physician or case manager involved in your care, or we may consult with an outside provider about your treatment.

### 2. Payment
We may use and disclose your PHI to bill and receive payment from you, your insurance company, Medicaid, Medicare, or another payer. For example, we may include information about your diagnosis and services when submitting a claim to your insurer.

### 3. Healthcare Operations
We may use and disclose your PHI for activities necessary to operate our organization. This includes quality assessment, staff training and supervision, compliance reviews, and business planning. Clinical supervisors may review records for quality purposes on a confidential basis.

### 4. Appointment Reminders
We may contact you by phone, email, or text to remind you of upcoming appointments or to provide information about treatment alternatives. You may request that we contact you in a specific way or at a specific location.

### 5. As Required by Law
We will disclose your PHI when required to do so by federal, state, or local law, including valid court orders, lawful subpoenas, and government investigations.

### 6. Mandatory Reporting — Abuse and Neglect
Our providers are mandated reporters. We are required by Florida law to report known or reasonably suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a child, elderly person, or vulnerable adult to the Florida Department of Children and Families (DCF) or law enforcement, regardless of your consent.

### 7. Imminent Risk of Harm — Duty to Protect
If you communicate a credible, serious threat of harm to yourself or another identifiable person, we may be required by law and professional ethics to take protective action, which may include warning the potential victim, notifying law enforcement, or arranging for emergency hospitalization.

### 8. Public Health Activities
We may disclose PHI to public health authorities authorized to collect information for the purpose of preventing or controlling disease, injury, or disability.

### 9. Judicial and Administrative Proceedings
We may disclose PHI in response to a valid court order, subpoena, or administrative request, subject to applicable legal protections.

### 10. Law Enforcement
We may disclose PHI to law enforcement officials in specific circumstances, such as reporting certain types of wounds, responding to a court order or administrative subpoena, or identifying a suspect or missing person.

### 11. Coroners and Medical Examiners
We may disclose PHI to a coroner or medical examiner to identify a deceased person or determine cause of death.

### 12. Workers' Compensation
We may disclose PHI to the extent authorized by and necessary to comply with workers' compensation laws or other similar programs.

### 13. Serious Threats to Health or Safety
We may use or disclose PHI if necessary to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat to the health or safety of a person or the public, provided the disclosure is made to someone reasonably able to prevent or lessen the threat.

## Uses and Disclosures Requiring Your Written Authorization

For uses and disclosures not described above, we will ask for your written authorization. You may revoke your authorization at any time in writing, except to the extent that we have already taken action in reliance on it. The following require your authorization:

- **Psychotherapy notes** — Notes recorded by a mental health professional that are kept separately from the rest of your record.
- **Marketing** — We will not use your PHI for marketing purposes without your written authorization.
- **Sale of PHI** — We will not sell your PHI.
- **Most disclosures of HIV/AIDS-related information** under Florida Statute §381.004.
- **Substance abuse treatment records** protected under 42 CFR Part 2.

## Your Rights Regarding Your Health Information

### Right to Access Your Records
You have the right to inspect and request a copy of your PHI. We will provide access within 30 days of your request (or 60 days if the records are maintained offsite). We may charge a reasonable fee for copies. To request access, submit a written request to our Privacy Officer.

### Right to Request Amendment
You have the right to request that we amend your PHI if you believe it is incorrect or incomplete. We may deny the request if the information was not created by us, is not part of the designated record set, or is already accurate. If we deny your request, you may submit a written statement of disagreement.

### Right to an Accounting of Disclosures
You have the right to request a list of certain disclosures of your PHI made in the six years prior to your request. This accounting does not include disclosures made for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations, disclosures made directly to you, or disclosures made pursuant to your authorization.

### Right to Request Restrictions
You have the right to request restrictions on how we use or disclose your PHI for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations, or to restrict disclosure to family members. We are not required to agree to your request except in the case of disclosures to a health plan for services you have paid for in full out-of-pocket.

### Right to Request Confidential Communications
You have the right to request that we communicate with you in a specific way or at a specific location. For example, you may ask that we contact you only at a specific phone number or address. We will accommodate reasonable requests.

### Right to a Paper Copy of This Notice
You have the right to a paper copy of this Notice at any time, even if you have agreed to receive it electronically.

### Right to Be Notified of a Breach
You have the right to be notified if your unsecured PHI is breached. We will provide notification without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery of the breach.
