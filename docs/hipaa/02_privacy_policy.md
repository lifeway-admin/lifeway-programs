## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the standards and procedures by which Lifeway Programs, Inc. ("Lifeway Programs") protects the privacy of Protected Health Information (PHI) in compliance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Parts 160 and 164). All workforce members who access, use, or disclose PHI are governed by this policy.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees, contractors, volunteers, interns, and board members of Lifeway Programs.
- All PHI in any format: electronic (ePHI), paper, and verbal.
- All systems and applications used to store or process PHI, including the Lifeway Programs CRM, booking platform, and any third-party systems.

---

## 3. Definitions

**Protected Health Information (PHI):** Individually identifiable health information that relates to an individual's past, present, or future physical or mental health, provision of healthcare, or payment for healthcare.

**Designated Record Set:** The group of records maintained by Lifeway Programs that is used to make decisions about individuals. Includes medical and billing records.

**Workforce:** Employees, volunteers, trainees, and other persons whose conduct is under the direct control of Lifeway Programs, whether or not they are paid.

**Privacy Officer:** The designated individual responsible for oversight of HIPAA privacy compliance.

**Covered Functions:** Activities that make Lifeway Programs a covered entity — the provision of healthcare and related payment and operations activities.

---

## 4. Privacy Officer

Lifeway Programs shall designate a Privacy Officer who is responsible for:

- Developing and implementing privacy policies and procedures;
- Conducting and overseeing workforce training;
- Receiving and addressing privacy complaints and questions;
- Conducting risk assessments related to privacy;
- Responding to requests by individuals to exercise their HIPAA rights;
- Coordinating breach notification activities;
- Maintaining required documentation.

The Privacy Officer shall be identified in the Notice of Privacy Practices and accessible to all patients and workforce members.

---

## 5. Minimum Necessary Standard

Workforce members shall access, use, and disclose only the minimum amount of PHI necessary to accomplish the intended purpose. This applies to all uses and disclosures, except:

- Disclosures to or requests by a healthcare provider for treatment;
- Disclosures made to the individual;
- Disclosures made pursuant to a valid authorization;
- Disclosures made to HHS for compliance and enforcement.

Workforce members shall not browse client records out of curiosity or for purposes unrelated to their job functions.

---

## 6. Permitted Uses and Disclosures

### 6.1 Treatment, Payment, and Healthcare Operations (TPO)
PHI may be used and disclosed for TPO without patient authorization as described in the Notice of Privacy Practices.

### 6.2 Mandatory Disclosures
PHI shall be disclosed:

- To the individual upon request;
- To HHS for compliance and enforcement activities;
- As required by Florida and federal mandatory reporting laws (child abuse, elder abuse, vulnerable adult abuse);
- When there is a credible, imminent threat to the life or safety of the patient or another identifiable person (Tarasoff-type duty to warn/protect).

### 6.3 Incidental Disclosures
Incidental disclosures that occur as a byproduct of a permitted use or disclosure are permissible, provided that reasonable safeguards are in place. Examples include other patients overhearing a conversation. Workforce members shall take reasonable precautions to minimize incidental disclosures.

### 6.4 Disclosures to Family Members or Others Involved in Care
PHI may be disclosed to a family member, relative, or close personal friend if:

- The patient is present and either agrees, does not object, or it can be reasonably inferred the patient would not object; or
- The patient is not present or is incapacitated, and the disclosure is in the patient's best interest in the professional judgment of the provider.

---

## 7. Uses and Disclosures Requiring Written Authorization

A valid HIPAA authorization is required before PHI may be used or disclosed for:

- Psychotherapy notes (separately maintained);
- Marketing;
- Sale of PHI;
- HIV-related information (Florida Statute §381.004);
- Substance abuse treatment records (42 CFR Part 2);
- Any other purpose not permitted under this policy.

Authorizations must include the required elements under 45 CFR §164.508. Lifeway Programs shall maintain a copy of each signed authorization and honor revocations promptly.

---

## 8. Patient Rights

Lifeway Programs shall establish and follow procedures for patients to exercise the following rights:

| Right | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Access to records | Written request; respond within 30 days; reasonable fee permissible |
| Amendment of records | Written request; respond within 60 days; document denials |
| Accounting of disclosures | Written request; provide list of disclosures back 6 years |
| Request restrictions | Written request; agree to restrictions on disclosures to health plans for self-pay services |
| Confidential communications | Accommodate reasonable requests |
| Copy of NPP | Provide upon request at no charge |

All patient rights requests shall be logged and tracked by the Privacy Officer.

---

## 9. Safeguards

Lifeway Programs shall implement the following safeguards to protect PHI:

**Administrative:** Workforce training, access controls, sanction policy, risk analysis.

**Physical:** Secure workstations, locked file cabinets for paper records, visitor access controls, screen positioning to prevent unauthorized viewing.

**Technical:** Unique user logins, automatic session timeouts, audit logging of all PHI access, encrypted data in transit (HTTPS/TLS), no-cache headers on PHI responses.

---

## 10. Verification of Requestors

Before disclosing PHI to a third party (excluding treatment providers), workforce members shall verify the identity and authority of the requestor. Acceptable verification includes:

- Government-issued photo ID;
- A copy of a valid authorization signed by the patient;
- A valid court order or legal process document;
- Credentials for law enforcement officers.

---

## 11. Business Associates

Lifeway Programs shall enter into a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with any third-party vendor, contractor, or entity that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of Lifeway Programs. A BAA must be in place before PHI is shared with or accessible to the business associate.

The Privacy Officer shall maintain a current list of business associates and ensure BAAs are in place and current.

---

## 12. Complaints

Patients and workforce members may submit privacy complaints to the Privacy Officer at jeffrey.tjok@proton.me. Complaints may also be submitted to the HHS Office for Civil Rights at hhs.gov/ocr. Lifeway Programs shall not retaliate against any individual for filing a privacy complaint.

All complaints shall be documented and investigated promptly. The Privacy Officer shall maintain records of all complaints and their resolution.

---

## 13. Training

All workforce members with access to PHI shall complete HIPAA privacy training within 30 days of hire and annually thereafter. Training completion shall be documented and records maintained for six years. See the Workforce Training Policy for details.

---

## 14. Sanctions

Workforce members who violate this policy shall be subject to sanctions as described in the Lifeway Programs Sanction Policy. Sanctions shall be applied consistently regardless of the seniority of the individual.

---

## 15. Documentation and Retention

All policies and procedures, training records, complaint logs, authorization forms, and records of disclosures shall be retained for a minimum of six (6) years from the date of creation or the date when last in effect, whichever is later.

---

## 16. Policy Review

This policy shall be reviewed annually by the Privacy Officer and updated as needed to reflect changes in law, regulation, or organizational practice.
