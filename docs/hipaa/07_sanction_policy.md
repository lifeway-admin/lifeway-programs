## 1. Purpose

The HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR §164.530(e)) and HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR §164.308(a)(1)(ii)(C)) require covered entities to apply appropriate sanctions against workforce members who fail to comply with their privacy and security policies. This policy establishes those sanctions, ensures consistent application, and documents the procedures for investigation and disciplinary action.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all Lifeway Programs workforce members — including employees, contractors, volunteers, interns, students, and board members — who use, disclose, access, or handle Protected Health Information (PHI) or electronic PHI (ePHI) in any format.

---

## 3. Categories of Violations

### Category 1 — Inadvertent Violation
An unintentional violation by a workforce member who is otherwise acting in good faith within the scope of their authority.

**Examples:**
- Sending a routine communication to the wrong email address without PHI being at serious risk;
- Leaving a screen visible in a low-risk setting for a brief period;
- Forgetting to log out of a workstation that is in a secured area.

### Category 2 — Negligent Violation
A violation resulting from failure to follow known policies, lack of due care, or disregard of training received, but without intentional wrongdoing.

**Examples:**
- Sharing login credentials with a coworker;
- Discussing patient information in a public area where others can overhear;
- Accessing PHI for a patient not under one's care without clinical justification;
- Failing to report a suspected breach in a timely manner;
- Using a personal, unencrypted device to store PHI.

### Category 3 — Intentional / Malicious Violation
A deliberate unauthorized access, use, or disclosure of PHI, or a violation motivated by personal gain, malice, or gross disregard for patient rights.

**Examples:**
- Accessing records of a family member, public figure, or personal acquaintance out of curiosity;
- Sharing patient information with an unauthorized third party;
- Selling or providing PHI without authorization;
- Accessing PHI after termination of employment;
- Deliberate circumvention of security controls;
- Filing false documentation to conceal a breach.

---

## 4. Sanctions by Category

Sanctions are progressive within each category and escalate based on severity, history, and impact. The Privacy/Security Officer and Executive Director shall determine the appropriate sanction in consultation with applicable legal counsel when warranted.

### Category 1 — Inadvertent
| Occurrence | Sanction |
|---|---|
| First | Verbal counseling; documented in personnel file; additional training as needed |
| Second | Written warning; mandatory retraining; documentation |
| Third or more | Treated as Category 2 |

### Category 2 — Negligent
| Occurrence | Sanction |
|---|---|
| First | Written warning; mandatory retraining; supervised access period |
| Second | Suspension without pay (1–5 days); formal performance improvement plan |
| Third or more | Termination of employment or engagement |

### Category 3 — Intentional / Malicious
| Occurrence | Sanction |
|---|---|
| Any | Immediate suspension pending investigation; termination upon finding of violation; referral to law enforcement and/or HHS Office for Civil Rights as appropriate |

## 5. Investigation Procedure

1. **Discovery:** Any workforce member, patient, or third party may report a suspected violation to the Privacy Officer or Security Officer.

2. **Initial Assessment (within 2 business days):** The Privacy/Security Officer shall conduct a preliminary review to assess the nature and scope of the potential violation.

3. **Formal Investigation (within 10 business days):** The Privacy/Security Officer shall gather relevant documentation, interview witnesses, review audit logs, and determine:
   - Whether a violation occurred;
   - The category of the violation;
   - The extent of PHI affected;
   - Whether the violation constitutes a breach requiring notification.

4. **Determination:** The Privacy/Security Officer shall issue a written determination, including the category of violation and recommended sanction.

5. **Sanction Application:** The Executive Director shall apply the sanction in coordination with HR. Workforce member shall be notified in writing.

6. **Documentation:** All investigation records, findings, and sanctions shall be retained for a minimum of six (6) years.

---

## 6. Appeals

A workforce member who believes a sanction was applied unfairly may appeal in writing to the Executive Director within 10 business days of receiving the sanction notice. The Executive Director shall respond in writing within 15 business days. The Executive Director's decision is final.

---

## 7. No Retaliation

No workforce member shall be retaliated against, intimidated, threatened, coerced, or discriminated against for reporting a HIPAA violation in good faith, filing a complaint, participating in an investigation, or opposing any act or practice believed to violate HIPAA.

Retaliation against a whistleblower is itself a HIPAA violation and will be subject to sanctions up to and including termination.
