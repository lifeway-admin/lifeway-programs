## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes procedures for identifying, assessing, documenting, and reporting breaches of Unsecured Protected Health Information (PHI) in compliance with the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule (45 CFR Part 164, Subpart D) and the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health (HITECH) Act.

---

## 2. Definitions

**Breach:** The acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted by the HIPAA Privacy Rule that compromises the security or privacy of the PHI. A breach is presumed unless the covered entity demonstrates that there is a low probability that the PHI has been compromised, based on a four-factor risk assessment.

**Unsecured PHI:** PHI that is not rendered unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable to unauthorized persons through the use of approved technologies (encryption or destruction per NIST standards).

**Business Associate:** A person or entity that performs functions or services on behalf of Lifeway Programs that involve the use or disclosure of PHI.

**Discovery:** A breach is considered "discovered" as of the first day on which the breach is known to Lifeway Programs, or by exercising reasonable diligence would have been known.

---

## 3. Exceptions — What Is NOT a Breach

The following situations are not considered breaches under HIPAA:

1. **Unintentional acquisition or access** by a workforce member acting in good faith and within the scope of authority, if the PHI is not further used or disclosed impermissibly.
2. **Inadvertent disclosure** by one authorized person to another authorized person within the same organization, if the PHI is not further used or disclosed impermissibly.
3. **Good-faith belief** that the unauthorized person to whom the disclosure was made would not have been able to retain the information.

---

## 4. Four-Factor Risk Assessment

When a potential breach is identified, the Privacy Officer shall conduct a risk assessment to determine whether there is a low probability of compromise. The four required factors are:

1. **Nature and extent of PHI involved** — the types of identifiers (name, SSN, diagnosis, financial), the sensitivity of the data (mental health records are especially sensitive), and the likelihood of re-identification.

2. **The unauthorized person who used the PHI or to whom the disclosure was made** — was it another healthcare provider, a trusted workforce member, or an external bad actor?

3. **Whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed** — evidence that PHI was or was not actually accessed (e.g., audit logs, system evidence).

4. **The extent to which the risk to PHI has been mitigated** — steps taken to recover or destroy the PHI and obtain reliable assurances that it will not be used or disclosed.

If the risk assessment does not demonstrate a low probability of compromise on all four factors, the incident is a breach requiring notification.

## 5. Breach Response Procedure

### Step 1 — Immediate Containment (Day 0–1)
- Contain the breach: revoke access, secure the system, disable compromised credentials.
- Preserve evidence: do not wipe logs or devices without direction from legal counsel.
- Notify the Privacy Officer and Security Officer immediately.

### Step 2 — Initial Assessment (Day 1–2)
- Identify what PHI was accessed, used, or disclosed.
- Identify the individuals whose PHI may be affected.
- Determine how the breach occurred.

### Step 3 — Risk Assessment (Day 2–5)
- Conduct the four-factor risk assessment.
- Document findings in writing.
- Determine whether the incident is a reportable breach.

### Step 4 — Documentation
- Complete a Breach Incident Report (see Section 7).
- Retain all documentation for six (6) years.

### Step 5 — Notification (if reportable breach)
Proceed with notifications as described in Section 6 below.

## 6. Notification Requirements

### 6.1 Notification to Affected Individuals
**Timeline:** Without unreasonable delay, no later than **60 calendar days** after discovery.

**Method:**
- First class mail to the individual's last known address; or
- If the individual has agreed to electronic notice, by email.

**If contact information is insufficient for fewer than 10 individuals:** Alternative means such as phone.

**If contact information is insufficient for 10 or more individuals:** Prominent posting on the Lifeway Programs website (lifewayprograms.org) for 90 days AND a toll-free number active for 90 days.

**Content of Individual Notice must include:**
- A brief description of what happened (dates);
- A description of the types of PHI involved;
- Steps individuals can take to protect themselves;
- A brief description of what Lifeway Programs is doing to investigate, mitigate, and protect against future occurrences;
- Contact information for a Lifeway Programs representative.

### 6.2 Notification to HHS
**Timeline:** All breaches must be reported to HHS.

- **Breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals:** Submit to HHS no later than **60 days after the end of the calendar year** in which the breach was discovered (annual log).
- **Breaches affecting 500 or more individuals:** Notify HHS simultaneously with individual notification — within 60 calendar days of discovery.

**Method:** Online at hhs.gov/hipaa (HHS Breach Reporting Tool).

### 6.3 Notification to Prominent Media Outlets
**Applicable when:** A breach affects **500 or more residents of a single state or jurisdiction**.

**Timeline:** Without unreasonable delay, no later than **60 calendar days** after discovery.

**Method:** Notice to prominent media outlets serving the state (e.g., major newspaper, television station).

### 6.4 Notification from Business Associates
If Lifeway Programs is notified by a Business Associate of a breach, Lifeway Programs remains responsible for providing notification to individuals. Business Associates must notify Lifeway Programs within **five (5) calendar days** of discovery per the BAA.

## 8. Annual Breach Log

For breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals, the Privacy Officer shall maintain a running log of all breaches throughout the calendar year for annual HHS submission. The log shall include the date of breach, date of discovery, description, number of individuals affected, and type of PHI involved.

---

## 9. Workforce Training

All workforce members shall be trained on how to identify and report suspected breaches. Breach scenarios shall be included in annual HIPAA training. Workforce members must report suspected breaches to the Privacy Officer **immediately upon discovery** — delaying a report to the Privacy Officer is itself a policy violation subject to sanctions.

---

## 10. Policy Review

This policy shall be reviewed annually and updated as necessary. It shall also be reviewed following any breach or significant near-miss incident.
